\begin{abstract}
Deep vision models can classify images accurately, but their accuracy does not
reveal how internal spatial representations develop into class-discriminative
decisions. Most visual explanation methods localize evidence for a chosen
output without directly characterizing the representation changes that precede
it. This PhD project investigates a complementary, representation-centred
hypothesis: trained vision models contain transition regimes in which already
decodable class information is reorganized into a geometry that can support
both explanation and knowledge transfer.

The first research stage uses dataset-wide, layer-specific principal component
analysis (PCA). Its first three coordinates are rendered as red, green, and blue
(PCA-RGB) maps. Train-fitted bases, calibration-only transition selection,
held-out evaluation, architecture-matched random controls, label-permutation
nulls, and distance-sensitivity tests establish a late transition in the tested
ConvNeXt models. Across the focused Pre-LN to Post-LN operation, PCA-128
cosine-prototype accuracy increases by $0.5948$ on CIFAR-10 and $0.5719$ on
CIFAR-100, while linear-probe Top-1 changes by only $0.0032$ and $0.0046$.
Effective rank rises from $1.65$ to $50.79$ and from $1.47$ to $88.53$.
\textbf{The transition therefore reorganizes existing class information rather
than marking its first appearance.} The same transition signature remains
measurable on ImageNet-1K, although compact class-prototype structure is weaker.

The second stage develops Adaptive K Dataset-Global Robust Prototypes
(K-DGRP), a class-agnostic, gradient-free explanation method that scores spatial
features by distance from dataset-global robust modes. Completed internal
experiments show competitive object localization on ResNet-50 and four held-out
convolutional neural network (CNN) families. Class-conditioned class activation
mapping methods remain stronger on some architectures, and expanded
faithfulness validation is still required.

The third stage studies knowledge distillation (KD), in which a compact student
learns from a stronger teacher. It introduces \jmethod{}, the project name for
a selective hierarchical geometry-distillation objective. Rather than copying
every teacher feature, \jmethod{} transfers an early relational state, balanced
principal-component coordinates 1--32 at selected transition and late layers,
and late affinity flow. On ImageNet-100, \jmethod{} attains
$94.947\pm0.205\%$ Top-1 accuracy across three seeds and is practically tied
with ReviewKD-120 under the current evidence.

\textbf{The immediate next stage is to complete and submit the \methodname{}
and \jmethod{} papers.} Transformer investigation will follow these publication
efforts through a broad literature review, a reproducible pilot, and
evidence-gated selection of the most informative architecture and explanatory
object.
\end{abstract}
