\begin{abstract}
Deep vision models can classify images accurately while leaving unclear how
their internal spatial representations develop into class-discriminative
decisions. Most class activation mapping methods localize evidence for a chosen
output, but do not directly characterize the representational changes that
precede that output. This PhD project investigates a complementary,
representation-centric hypothesis: trained vision models contain a transition
regime in which generic visual features are reorganized into class-relevant
spatial geometry, and this geometry can be used both to explain models and to
transfer knowledge.

The first research stage uses dataset-wide, layer-specific principal-component
bases to visualize convolutional representations as PCA-RGB maps. Preliminary
observations distinguish early feature-preserving states, intermediate
transition states, and late class-oriented states. Trained-versus-random
controls and CIFAR-10 misclassification examples support further investigation,
while the weaker visual regularity on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 motivates
quantitative rather than purely visual transition criteria. The second stage
develops \methodname, a class-agnostic, gradient-free explanation method that
scores spatial features by distance from dataset-global robust modes. Completed
internal experiments show competitive object localization on ResNet-50 and
four held-out CNN families after selector development on ResNet and ConvNeXt,
although class-conditioned CAMs remain stronger on some architectures and
additional faithfulness validation is required. The third stage studies
knowledge distillation (KD), in which a compact student learns from a stronger
teacher. It introduces \jmethod{}, the project name for a selective hierarchical
geometry-distillation objective. Rather than copying every teacher feature,
\jmethod{} transfers an early relational state, balanced PC1--32 transition
coordinates, and late affinity flow. On ImageNet-100, \jmethod{} attains
$94.947\pm0.205\%$ Top-1 accuracy across three seeds and is practically tied
with ReviewKD-120 under the current evidence.

\textbf{The immediate next stage is to complete and submit two papers: one on
\methodname{} and one on \jmethod{}.} Transformer investigation will follow
these publication efforts and remains intentionally flexible until a focused
literature review and reproducible pilot study identify the most informative
architectures and explanatory objects.
\end{abstract}
