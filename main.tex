\begin{abstract}
Deep vision models can classify images accurately, but their accuracy does not
reveal how internal spatial representations develop into class-discriminative
decisions. Most class activation mapping (CAM) methods localize evidence for a
chosen output without directly characterizing the representational changes that
precede it. This PhD project investigates a complementary
representation-centric hypothesis. Trained vision models may contain a
transition regime in which generic visual features are reorganized into
class-relevant spatial geometry that can support both explanation and knowledge
transfer.

The first research stage uses dataset-wide, layer-specific principal component
analysis (PCA). Its first three coordinates are rendered as red, green, and blue
(PCA-RGB) maps. Preliminary observations distinguish early feature-preserving
states, intermediate transition states, and late class-oriented states.
Comparisons between trained and random networks, together with CIFAR-10
misclassification examples, support further investigation. The weaker visual
regularity on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 motivates quantitative rather than
purely visual transition criteria. The second stage
develops Adaptive K Dataset-Global Robust Prototypes (K-DGRP), a class-agnostic,
gradient-free explanation method that
scores spatial features by distance from dataset-global robust modes. Completed
internal experiments show competitive object localization on ResNet-50 and
four held-out convolutional neural network (CNN) families. The selector was
developed on ResNet and ConvNeXt before evaluation on the held-out families.
Class-conditioned CAMs remain stronger on some architectures, and further
faithfulness validation is still required. The third stage studies knowledge
distillation (KD), in which a compact student learns from a stronger
teacher. It introduces \jmethod{}, the project name for a selective hierarchical
geometry-distillation objective. Rather than copying every teacher feature,
\jmethod{} transfers an early relational state, balanced principal-component
coordinates 1--32 (PC1--32) at selected transition and late layers, and late
affinity flow. On ImageNet-100, \jmethod{} attains
$94.947\pm0.205\%$ Top-1 accuracy across three seeds and is practically tied
with ReviewKD-120 under the current evidence.

\textbf{The immediate next stage is to complete and submit two papers: one on
\methodname{} and one on \jmethod{}.} Transformer investigation will follow
these publication efforts. A focused literature review and reproducible pilot
will first survey the broad design space. The project will then concentrate on
the most informative architectures and explanatory objects.
\end{abstract}
