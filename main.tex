\begin{abstract}
Deep vision models can classify images accurately, but their accuracy does not
reveal how their internal representations become organized for a decision.
This PhD investigates whether the geometry that develops inside vision models
can support both \textbf{explanation and knowledge transfer.}

The first stage uses principal component analysis with red--green--blue
rendering (PCA-RGB) to follow spatial representations through a convolutional
neural network (CNN). The experiments reveal a late transition pattern: class
information is already readable before the transition, but is then reorganized
into a more compact, class-oriented geometry. Comparisons with
architecture-matched randomly initialized networks, label-permutation tests,
and basis-invariant measurements support this pattern on CIFAR-10 and
CIFAR-100, with a related but weaker signature on ImageNet-1K.

The second stage develops Adaptive K Dataset-Global Robust Prototypes
(K-DGRP), a class-agnostic and gradient-free visual explanation method. Across
six CNN architectures, the results support competitive object-relevant
localization relative to class activation mapping (CAM) baselines. The
full-dimensional RAW method remains the canonical version.

The third stage studies knowledge distillation (KD), in which a compact student
learns from a stronger teacher. It introduces \jmethod{}, a selective
hierarchical geometry-distillation objective. On ImageNet-100, \jmethod{}
achieves $94.947\pm0.205\%$ Top-1 accuracy across three seeds and is practically
tied with ReviewKD-120 under the current evidence.
\end{abstract}
