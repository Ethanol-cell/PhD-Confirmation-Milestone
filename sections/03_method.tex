\section{Research Framework and Methodology}\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Overall design}

The project uses a staged empirical design. Exploratory visualization generates
hypotheses, but confirmatory claims require frozen metrics, held-out data,
matched baselines, and explicit controls. The same core object---spatial
representation geometry---is examined in three roles:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item as a descriptive object in dataset-wide PCA-RGB analysis;
    \item as an explanatory object in \methodname{}; and
    \item as a transferable training object in \jmethod{}.
\end{enumerate}

The stages are related but not interchangeable. A visually coherent PCA map is
not automatically an attribution. A localization map is not automatically
class-causal. A distillation gain establishes training utility but does not
prove that the corresponding visualization faithfully explains an individual
decision.

\subsection{Datasets, models, and controls}

The representation study currently spans CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and
ImageNet-100 \cite{krizhevsky2009cifar,deng2009imagenet}. CIFAR-10 provides a
low-class-count setting in which class-level organization is visually
inspectable. CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 test whether observations survive
greater class diversity and intra-class variation. CNN experiments include
ResNet \cite{he2016resnet}, ConvNeXt \cite{liu2022convnext}, VGG, RegNet,
MobileNet, and EfficientNet families where appropriate.

Controls include randomly initialized networks with the same architecture,
repeated trained seeds, correct and incorrect predictions, and matched semantic
taps. The random control separates structure induced by architecture from
structure learned through supervised training. It does not, by itself, prove
that one intermediate layer is causally necessary.

\subsection{Dataset-wide layer-specific PCA-RGB}

For layer $\ell$, image $x_i$, and spatial position $p$, let
\begin{equation}
    \bm f^{(\ell)}_{i,p}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_\ell}
\end{equation}
be the channel vector. A dataset-wide reservoir
$\mathcal{F}_\ell=\{\bm f^{(\ell)}_{i,p}\}$, constructed by sampling spatial
vectors across the training split, is used to fit mean
$\bm\mu_\ell$ and orthonormal PCA basis
$\bm V_\ell=[\bm v_{\ell,1},\bm v_{\ell,2},\ldots]$
\cite{pearson1901pca,hotelling1933pca}. The displayed
coordinates are
\begin{equation}
    \bm z^{(\ell)}_{i,p}
    = (\bm f^{(\ell)}_{i,p}-\bm\mu_\ell)
      \bm V_{\ell,1:3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},
    \qquad
    \bm C^{(\ell)}_{i,p}=\phi_\ell(\bm z^{(\ell)}_{i,p}),
    \label{eq:pca-rgb}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_\ell$ is a frozen layer-specific display transform mapping the
three coordinates to RGB.

All images at a given layer share the same basis; therefore within-layer colour
comparisons are meaningful after the sign convention and display transform are
frozen. Each layer has its own basis, so a red region at layer $\ell$ is not
automatically the same feature direction as a red region at layer
$\ell+1$. Cross-depth conclusions will use basis-invariant metrics or explicit
subspace alignment rather than colour identity alone.

\subsection{Operationalizing a representation transition}

A transition layer should not be selected only because its visualization looks
interesting. The planned operational definition combines:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{semantic maturity:} linear-probe accuracy, logit-decoding
    agreement, class-prototype accuracy, or between/within-class separation;
    \item \textbf{spatial structure:} object localization, boundary quality,
    foreground enrichment, and spatial entropy;
    \item \textbf{representational change:} adjacent-layer CKA, aligned
    subspace change, affinity change, or a preregistered change-point score; and
    \item \textbf{robustness:} agreement across seeds, datasets, and reasonable
    choices of PCA dimension and layer sampling.
\end{itemize}

A candidate transition regime is where semantic maturity rises while
object-relevant spatial variation remains available. This avoids assuming that
the last convolution or final activation is universally optimal.

\subsection{Explanation evaluation}

\methodname{} is evaluated at native spatial resolution and then bilinearly
upsampled. Exact-area IoU uses the top $q$ fraction of pixels:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{IoU}@q
    =\frac{|T_q(M)\cap G|}{|T_q(M)\cup G|},
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the continuous map and $G$ is the object mask. The primary
internal setting is $q=0.20$. AUPRC evaluates the complete ranking and is
informative when foreground pixels are the minority class
\cite{davis2006prroc,saito2015precisionrecall}; boundary F1 measures contour
agreement. Causal deletion replaces highly ranked pixels
with a frozen perturbation and measures predicted-class margin loss. Paired
bootstrap confidence intervals resample images rather than treating pixels as
independent \cite{efron1993bootstrap}.

The paper-ready rerun will add insertion, class sensitivity, parameter
randomization, and correct-versus-incorrect analyses. This is particularly
important because \methodname{} is class-agnostic: its natural claim is
object-relevant representation distinctiveness, not attribution to an arbitrary
target class.

\subsection{Knowledge-distillation evaluation}

All teacher-derived PCA, RMS, hash, and gradient-calibration assets are fitted
on internal training data only. Teacher and student receive exactly the same
stochastic augmented tensor so that spatial coordinates refer to the same
visible content. The ImageNet-100 study reports Top-1, Top-5, negative
log-likelihood (NLL), expected calibration error (ECE), seed count, and
checkpoint selection. Multi-seed means and standard deviations are used when
available. Single-seed external baselines are descriptive and do not support
formal superiority claims. ECE is treated as a separate calibration measure
rather than a substitute for accuracy \cite{guo2017calibration}.

\subsection{Reproducibility and threats to validity}

The main threats are post-hoc layer selection, PCA sign or scaling instability,
using the same cohort for method development and confirmation, augmentation
misalignment, unequal training budgets, and overinterpreting single-seed
baselines. To address them, the final studies will separate calibration,
development, and sealed evaluation roles; freeze selectors before accessing
ground truth; preserve exact configuration hashes; and report failed or
rejected variants as part of the evidence chain. Cross-dataset confirmation is
required before architecture-general observations are presented as
distribution-general findings.
