\section{Progress I: Representation-Transition Discovery}\label{sec:pca-progress}

\subsection{Qualitative depth progression}

Dataset-wide PCA-RGB atlases were constructed by applying
\cref{eq:pca-rgb} at multiple CNN states. Three recurring regimes were
observed. Early layers retain edges, colour contrast, and local texture. Middle
states exhibit stronger regional grouping and rapid changes in spatial
organization. Late states become compact and class-oriented, although the
degree to which this remains visually recognizable depends on the dataset.

This progression is provisionally described as
\begin{equation}
    \text{feature extraction}
    \longrightarrow
    \text{representation transition}
    \longrightarrow
    \text{class organization}.
\end{equation}
The labels are explanatory summaries rather than yet-frozen layer categories.
They will be validated using the semantic, spatial, and change measures in
\cref{sec:framework}.

\subsection{Randomly initialized control}

The random-network experiment shows that recognizable image structure can
survive into late CNN layers even without supervised learning. Convolutions,
residual paths, downsampling, and nonlinearities impose an architectural image
prior; visible structure at depth therefore cannot be interpreted as learned
semantics by itself. In the trained network, however, later features reorganize
into more class-discriminative patterns. The appropriate conclusion is that
architecture propagates structure while training changes its semantic
organization.

This control does not yet establish the location or causal importance of the
transition. A stronger test will compare multiple random initializations with
multiple trained seeds using the same layers and basis-fitting protocol.
Semantic probe accuracy, class separation, adjacent-layer change, and
foreground localization will determine whether the trained network departs
systematically from its random counterpart.

\subsection{CIFAR-10 class organization and errors}

CIFAR-10 provides the clearest qualitative class-level pattern. Because one
dataset-wide basis is reused for every image at a given layer, same-layer colour
organization can be compared across examples. Selected late-layer maps from the
same class appear similar, suggesting that the dominant feature coordinates
capture recurring class structure. In selected errors, the map appears closer
to the predicted class than to the ground-truth class. This is consistent with
the representation supporting the model's decision rather than merely encoding
the annotation.

The current wrong-prediction analysis is semantic and qualitative. A
confirmatory analysis will construct class prototypes from a training split,
then evaluate every held-out error. For representation descriptor
$\bm r_i^{(\ell)}$ and class prototype $\bm c_k^{(\ell)}$, the relevant
comparison is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_i^{(\ell)}
    = d(\bm r_i^{(\ell)},\bm c_{y_i}^{(\ell)})
      -d(\bm r_i^{(\ell)},\bm c_{\hat y_i}^{(\ell)}),
\end{equation}
where $y_i$ is the true class and $\hat y_i$ is the predicted class. Positive
$\Delta_i$ means the example is closer to the predicted-class prototype. The
analysis must use held-out images and include confidence intervals, confusion
pairs, prediction confidence, and failure cases.

\subsection{CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100}

On CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100, visually uniform same-class late-layer colours
are less obvious. It is tempting to attribute this directly to dataset
complexity, but several explanations remain confounded: class count,
intra-class variation, spatial resolution, model capacity, basis variance, and
distributed rather than prototype-like class geometry. The defensible present
finding is narrower: simple class-consistent colour organization weakens, while
intermediate spatial reorganization remains visible.

This actually strengthens the need for a representation-transition framework.
A useful transition should not depend on a human being able to assign a colour
to each class. Quantitative measures can detect increasing class organization
in a high-dimensional subspace even when a three-dimensional projection is
visually ambiguous. Neural-collapse diagnostics, for example, evaluate
within-class contraction and class-mean geometry without assuming that the
first three principal directions contain the complete class simplex.

\subsection{Current interpretation}

The completed observations motivate, but do not yet fully prove, RQ1. The most
important advance is the shift from treating one final heatmap as the entire
explanation to studying the formation of spatial geometry. The next evidence
must establish three points: a transition is reproducibly measurable; its
location is not selected retrospectively from the evaluation mask; and the
geometry at or after that transition carries information useful for
localization or transfer. The \methodname{} and \jmethod{} studies address the
last point from different directions.
