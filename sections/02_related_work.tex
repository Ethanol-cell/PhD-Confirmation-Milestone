\section{Critical Literature Review}\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Scope and terminology}

Explainability in machine learning is not one problem with one universally
correct output. The literature uses terms such as interpretability,
explainability, attribution, visualization, transparency, and understanding for
partly overlapping goals. Lipton distinguishes properties of intrinsically
transparent models from post-hoc explanations, while Rudin argues that, in
high-stakes settings, an interpretable model may be preferable to explaining an
unnecessarily opaque one \cite{lipton2018mythos,rudin2019stop}. Evaluation
frameworks also distinguish application-grounded studies with real users,
human-grounded proxy tasks, and functionally grounded tests that do not require
human participants \cite{doshivelez2017rigorous}. Broad surveys organize the
field by whether an explanation is local or global, model-specific or
model-agnostic, and intrinsic or post-hoc
\cite{guidotti2018survey,arrieta2020xai,samek2021explaining}.

These distinctions matter for this project. A saliency map can estimate input
importance for one score; a concept method can test sensitivity to a
human-defined direction; a prototype method can retrieve representative
examples; and a representation diagnostic can describe how internal geometry
changes with depth. All may be useful, but they do not make the same claim.
\textbf{This report therefore separates representation diagnostics,
attribution, and human-facing explanation.} PCA-RGB is primarily a
representation diagnostic. Adaptive K-DGRP is a class-agnostic spatial
explanation of representation distinctiveness. CAM methods are usually
class-conditioned attribution or localization methods. J1 is an intervention:
it tests whether selected internal geometry is useful for knowledge transfer.

The distinction also prevents a common category error. A visually plausible
map is not automatically faithful to a classifier, and a faithful sensitivity
map is not automatically understandable or useful to a person. The report uses
``explanation'' when a method connects an internal or input-space object to a
stated explanatory question, and it states the question explicitly. For
K-DGRP, that question is which spatial features are distinctive relative to
recurrent dataset-global modes. For a predicted-class CAM, it is which spatial
locations support a particular logit. The answers can overlap without being
equivalent.

\subsection{Gradient and relevance-propagation attribution}

One early family explains a network output through its local derivative with
respect to the input. Vanilla saliency uses the gradient of a class score with
respect to image pixels \cite{simonyan2014saliency}. Guided backpropagation
modifies backward flow through rectifiers to produce sharper visualizations
\cite{springenberg2015striving}, and SmoothGrad averages gradients under input
noise to reduce visual variance \cite{smilkov2017smoothgrad}. Integrated
Gradients accumulates derivatives along a path from a baseline and is motivated
by sensitivity and implementation-invariance axioms
\cite{sundararajan2017axiomatic}. DeepLIFT compares each activation with a
reference activation and propagates contribution differences
\cite{shrikumar2017deeplift}. Comparative analyses show that several of these
rules can be understood as closely related gradient-times-signal estimators
under different local approximations and reference choices
\cite{ancona2018gradient}.

Layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP) redistributes a prediction backward
through the network under conservation-style rules
\cite{bach2015pixelwise}. Deep Taylor decomposition derives related propagation
rules by locally expanding around selected root points
\cite{montavon2017deeptaylor}, and broader treatments connect Taylor,
relevance, gradient, and activation-maximization perspectives
\cite{montavon2018methods}. FullGrad augments input gradients with gradients
with respect to bias terms to account for the complete local representation of
a piecewise-linear network \cite{srinivas2019fullgrad}. These methods provide
fine spatial detail and are often inexpensive, but their output depends on
nonlinear saturation, reference or root choices, backward rules, and local
gradient behavior. Some propagation rules can produce nearly class-insensitive
or architecture-insensitive visual structure, and modified backpropagation may
detach the visualization from the actual forward computation
\cite{adebayo2018sanity,sixt2020explanationslie}.

\textbf{Sharpness is not the same as faithfulness.} A high-resolution map may
principally reconstruct edges or image structure, whereas a lower-resolution
map may more directly track the selected score. For this reason, the present
project does not choose an explanation method by appearance alone and includes
randomization, perturbation, and class-sensitivity tests in its publication
protocol.

\subsection{Perturbation, surrogate, and example-based explanations}

Perturbation methods estimate importance through controlled changes to the
input or internal representation. Occlusion analysis masks image regions and
measures the resulting score change \cite{zeiler2014visualizing}. Meaningful
Perturbation optimizes a deletion mask while regularizing its area and
smoothness \cite{fong2017meaningful}; Extremal Perturbations instead searches
for a smooth region of prescribed area that maximally preserves or changes the
target response \cite{fong2019extremal}. RISE samples randomized masks and
weights them by black-box output scores \cite{petsiuk2018rise}. Because these
approaches query the forward function, they can provide more direct behavioral
evidence than a visually attractive gradient. Their weakness is that masking,
blurring, inpainting, or replacing pixels can create out-of-distribution
inputs, so the result partly reflects the perturbation operator.

Model-agnostic surrogates approximate a complex predictor near an example.
LIME fits a sparse local linear model to perturbed samples
\cite{ribeiro2016lime}. SHAP connects additive feature attribution with
Shapley-value principles and identifies a common structure shared by several
attribution schemes \cite{lundberg2017shap}. These tools offer a clear local
feature-weight interpretation, but image explanations still depend on the
segmentation, background distribution, locality kernel, and feature-dependence
assumptions. Their explanatory object is the surrogate around the chosen
sample, not a complete account of how a deep representation formed.

Example-based explanations identify influential or representative training
cases rather than pixels. Influence functions approximate how upweighting a
training point changes parameters or a test prediction
\cite{koh2017influence}; representer points decompose a prediction into a
weighted combination of training representations under particular final-layer
and regularization conditions \cite{yeh2018representer}. Such methods are
valuable when users reason by analogy or need to inspect possible data errors,
but they answer a different question from spatial localization. They also
inherit sensitivity to training dynamics, representation choice, and
approximation assumptions.

\subsection{Concept, prototype, and representation-based explanation}

Concept-based methods move from individual pixels toward semantic units.
Network Dissection measures alignment between individual units and a labelled
concept vocabulary \cite{bau2017networkdissection}. TCAV defines a concept
activation vector from examples and measures directional sensitivity of a
class score to that vector \cite{kim2018tcav}. ACE automates candidate concept
discovery by segmenting and clustering image regions before applying a
concept-sensitivity test \cite{ghorbani2019ace}. Concept Bottleneck Models
place predicted concepts in the forward path so that a user can inspect or
intervene on them \cite{koh2020conceptbottleneck}. These methods can express
explanations in more human-recognizable terms, but discovered concepts depend
on annotations, exemplars, segmentation, layer choice, and the completeness of
the concept vocabulary.

Prototype networks make similarity to learned cases part of the prediction.
ProtoPNet, for example, compares image patches with learned class prototypes
and presents a ``this looks like that'' justification
\cite{chen2019protopnet}. Label-free representation explanation instead seeks
structure without prespecified semantic labels, including post-hoc description
of unsupervised representations \cite{crabbe2022labelfree}. Feature
visualization and activation maximization provide another view by synthesizing
or retrieving inputs that strongly activate units
\cite{yosinski2015deepvis}. These approaches motivate this project's interest
in recurrent spatial feature geometry, while also warning that a geometric
mode is not automatically a named semantic concept.

Adaptive K-DGRP lies between prototype analysis and spatial explanation. Its
robust centres summarize common modes of unpooled feature vectors across a
label-free calibration set; the explanation highlights locations that are far
from every such mode. The centres are not claimed to be human concepts, class
prototypes, or counterfactual examples. \textbf{The method explains spatial
distinctiveness relative to a learned representation distribution, not the
full causal mechanism of a class decision.}

\subsection{Class activation mapping}

Class activation mapping is especially influential in computer vision because
it connects semantic convolutional features with spatial localization. The
original CAM architecture uses global average pooling and classifier weights
to form a class-specific linear combination of feature maps
\cite{zhou2016cam}. Grad-CAM replaces the fixed classifier weights with
spatially pooled gradients and therefore applies to a broader range of CNNs
\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam}. Grad-CAM++ uses higher-order derivative terms to
better handle multiple occurrences, while XGrad-CAM derives weights from
axioms concerning sensitivity and conservation
\cite{chattopadhay2018gradcampp,fu2020xgradcam}. Score-CAM and Ablation-CAM
avoid direct gradient weights by measuring score changes after activation-map
masking or channel ablation
\cite{wang2020scorecam,desai2020ablationcam}. LayerCAM combines positive
element-wise gradients with activations and can exploit shallower spatial
resolution \cite{jiang2021layercam}. EigenCAM instead projects activations
onto a dominant principal direction and is class-agnostic
\cite{muhammad2020eigencam}.

CAM variants are useful because they are simple, spatially interpretable, and
often align with objects. Nevertheless, their explanation depends on a chosen
layer, target score, resolution, and weighting rule. The final convolutional
state has strong semantics but coarse spatial support; earlier layers preserve
detail but may be weakly class-specific. Layer aggregation can improve
coverage, but it does not by itself explain why one representation stage is
appropriate. Score-based variants reduce gradient pathologies but introduce
many forward evaluations and masking assumptions. Class-agnostic EigenCAM may
expose dominant variance rather than decision-relevant evidence.

This motivates a different starting point. Instead of assuming that the final
convolution is the explanatory layer, the project first studies how spatial
representation changes across depth. K-DGRP then asks whether the distribution
of semantic spatial features contains object-relevant structure without a
target class or gradient. The comparison with CAMs is therefore informative
but asymmetric. \textbf{Localization and classifier faithfulness are different
evaluation axes.}

\subsection{Limitations, faithfulness, and evaluation}

Several studies demonstrate that explanation quality cannot be inferred from
appearance. Model-parameter and data randomization tests reveal that some
saliency methods remain visually similar after learned parameters or labels are
destroyed \cite{adebayo2018sanity}. ROAR removes features judged important,
retrains the model, and evaluates the resulting loss of predictive information
to reduce artifacts from using a fixed model on corrupted inputs
\cite{hooker2019roar}. Infidelity measures disagreement between attribution and
output change under a perturbation distribution, while sensitivity measures
local explanation instability \cite{yeh2019infidelity}. Pixel-flipping and
region-perturbation protocols similarly test whether removing highly ranked
evidence changes the decision \cite{samek2017evaluating}.

Robustness is another concern. Small adversarial changes can alter an
interpretation without materially changing the prediction
\cite{ghorbani2019fragile}, and explanation maps can be manipulated through
the geometry of high-dimensional decision surfaces
\cite{dombrowski2019explanations}. Input transformations that leave the model
function unchanged can still change some attribution outputs, exposing
dependence on preprocessing or baseline choice
\cite{kindermans2019unreliability}. Metrics can also disagree because they
measure different properties: deletion asks whether selected evidence is
necessary under one corruption process, insertion asks whether it is
sufficient under another, localization compares with human object masks, and
pointing-game accuracy rewards only a peak location.

\textbf{No explanation family is uniformly faithful under every definition.}
The correct response is not to select one favorable metric, but to state the
intended explanatory object and assemble convergent tests. The K-DGRP paper
therefore requires exact-area IoU and AUPRC for localization; insertion and
deletion for behavioral response; class-sensitivity tests for the boundary
between objectness and target attribution; feature-norm and random-map
baselines; parameter and label randomization; stability across seeds and
architectures; and qualitative cases selected without looking at final metric
outcomes. Human-grounded evaluation may later test usefulness, but it should
not replace functional evidence.

\subsection{Representation evolution and neural collapse}

The project is also related to methods that compare internal
representations rather than attribute an output. Linear probes measure how
readily task information can be decoded at different depths
\cite{alain2017linearprobes}. Representational similarity analysis compares
pairwise dissimilarity structure between representational spaces
\cite{kriegeskorte2008rsa}. SVCCA and projection-weighted CCA align
subspaces learned by different layers, models, or training times
\cite{raghu2017svcca,morcos2018pwcca}. Centred kernel alignment (CKA) provides
an invariant similarity measure with favorable empirical behavior for neural
representations \cite{kornblith2019cka}. Other work asks whether separately
trained networks learn convergent features and emphasizes statistical testing
when interpreting similarity scores
\cite{li2015convergent,ding2021grounding}.

PCA-RGB is simpler and more spatially direct. Principal component analysis
finds orthogonal directions of maximal variance, originating in classical
work by Pearson and Hotelling \cite{pearson1901pca,hotelling1933pca}. Mapping
the first three scores to RGB makes a high-dimensional feature grid visually
inspectable. In this project, one dataset-wide basis is fitted per layer, so
images can be compared within that layer. Because each layer has its own basis,
colour is not aligned across depth; adjacent-layer claims must instead use
invariant or explicitly aligned quantities such as CKA, probe performance,
class scatter, affinity change, or subspace angles. PCA also privileges global
variance, which can reflect background, texture, feature norm, or sampling
imbalance rather than semantics.

Neural collapse provides a useful late-training reference. In the terminal
phase of balanced classification, within-class features can contract, class
means can approach a simplex equiangular tight frame, classifier weights can
align with class means, and nearest-class-centre decisions can agree with the
network \cite{papyan2020neuralcollapse}. Subsequent theory studies the same
geometry with unconstrained features and under mean-squared-error dynamics
\cite{zhu2021neuralcollapse,han2022neuralcollapse}. Layer-peeled analysis also
shows how imbalance can produce minority collapse, demonstrating that the
idealized balanced geometry is not universal \cite{fang2021layerpeeled}. A
recent review synthesizes definitions, theoretical models, and open questions
\cite{kothapalli2023neuralcollapse}.

\textbf{Neural collapse is a model of late class geometry, not an explanation
of every spatial prototype.} The K-DGRP centres summarize common unpooled
spatial modes and are few in number; they are not expected to reproduce a
100-class simplex. Neural-collapse diagnostics can nevertheless quantify when
class organization matures and help distinguish class-centroid structure from
the broader spatial/common-mode geometry used by K-DGRP. This is particularly
valuable on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100, where the first three principal
coordinates do not show an obvious class-consistent colour code.

\subsection{Knowledge distillation: response-based transfer}

Knowledge distillation (KD) transfers information from a teacher or ensemble
to a student. Early model-compression work trained a compact model on targets
generated by a larger ensemble \cite{bucila2006compression}, and later work
showed that shallow networks can learn the function of deeper models
\cite{ba2014deep}. The widely used formulation of Hinton et al. combines
ground-truth cross-entropy with a temperature-softened divergence between
teacher and student class distributions \cite{hinton2015distilling}. The soft
targets expose relative probabilities among non-target classes, sometimes
described as ``dark knowledge,'' and can supply a denser learning signal than
one-hot labels.

Response transfer has developed in several directions. Born-Again Networks
show that a student with the same architecture can improve through sequential
distillation \cite{furlanello2018ban}. Deep Mutual Learning removes the fixed
teacher and lets peer networks learn from one another
\cite{zhang2018dml}; on-the-fly native ensembles aggregate branches during a
single training process \cite{lan2018one}. Self-distillation applies auxiliary
classifiers or earlier generations within one architecture
\cite{zhang2019byot}, and self-supervised auxiliary tasks can enrich the
distillation signal \cite{xu2020sskd}. Decoupled KD separates target-class and
non-target-class contributions, showing that the conventional KL term combines
distinct forms of transfer \cite{zhao2022dkd}.

Output KD is attractive because teacher and student need only share a label
space, but it leaves internal spatial organization unspecified. Its behavior is
also conditional. A teacher with higher standalone accuracy is not always the
best teacher, and a large teacher--student capacity gap can make transfer
difficult; teacher-assistant distillation inserts an intermediate model to
bridge that gap \cite{mirzadeh2020takd}. Controlled studies report that the
benefit depends on teacher confidence, early stopping, augmentation, and the
relative learning capacities of teacher and student
\cite{cho2019efficacy,beyer2022patient}. Theoretical and empirical analyses
seek to explain which teacher information is learnable and whether a student
actually matches the teacher function away from the training samples
\cite{phuong2019understandingkd,stanton2021doeskd}. Surveys consequently
describe KD as a family spanning response, feature, relation, and online
transfer rather than one fixed loss \cite{gou2021kdsurvey,wang2021kdreview}.

\subsection{Feature, relation, and hierarchical distillation}

Feature distillation supplies intermediate supervision. FitNets use a learned
regressor to match a teacher hint layer and help optimize a thin student
\cite{romero2015fitnets}. Attention Transfer matches normalized spatial
attention summaries rather than raw channels \cite{zagoruyko2017attention}.
FSP matrices encode correlations between pairs of layers and transfer a form of
feature-flow structure \cite{yim2017fsp}. Factor Transfer paraphrases teacher
features into factors and trains a student translator to reproduce them
\cite{kim2018factortransfer}. These methods establish that useful knowledge
exists beyond logits, but they also introduce decisions about feature adapters,
layer correspondence, normalization, and loss scale.

Relational methods reduce dependence on channel identity. Relational KD
matches pairwise distances and angles among examples
\cite{park2019rkd}. Similarity-Preserving KD matches pairwise activation
similarities within a batch \cite{tung2019similarity}. Correlation congruence
transfers higher-order relations between instances
\cite{peng2019correlation}, while probabilistic and variational information
distillation frame transfer in distributional or information-theoretic terms
\cite{passalis2018probabilistic,ahn2019vid}. Contrastive Representation
Distillation uses a contrastive objective to distinguish corresponding
teacher--student representations from negatives \cite{tian2020crd}. Activation
Boundary transfer focuses on which side of a teacher neuron's activation
boundary the student occupies rather than forcing exact regression
\cite{heo2019activation}.

More recent feature methods address selectivity and cross-layer mismatch. OFD
uses margin-based partial feature transfer and teacher-derived transforms
\cite{heo2019ofd}. ReviewKD aggregates multi-level student features and
revisits teacher knowledge across levels \cite{chen2021reviewkd}. SemCKD learns
semantic calibration weights between possible teacher and student layer pairs
\cite{chen2021semckd}. SimKD aligns student features to a teacher classifier
that is reused at inference \cite{chen2022simkd}. MGD masks student features
and reconstructs teacher features through a generator
\cite{yang2022mgd}. CAT-KD transfers class-attention maps and emphasizes
class-relevant spatial structure \cite{guo2023catkd}. InDistill explicitly
targets information-flow preservation across intermediate stages
\cite{sarridis2025indistill}.

The literature supports two conclusions relevant to this thesis. First,
feature geometry, spatial attention, pairwise relations, and inter-layer change
can all carry transferable information. Second, copying more states is not
automatically better. Teacher and student can differ in depth, width,
downsampling schedule, receptive field, and optimization trajectory. An exact
coordinate constraint at every layer may suppress a student-specific efficient
path, while a relation loss can preserve useful organization without requiring
channel equality.

\subsection{KD limitations and positioning of J1}

The central unresolved design question is where to constrain a student and
where to let it differ. Automatic layer association can help, but association
scores do not guarantee that the selected knowledge object is causally useful.
Feature dimensions can be adapted, yet an adapter may absorb a mismatch rather
than transfer teacher structure. Large auxiliary coefficients can produce an
apparent method gain that is actually a gradient-scale effect. A teacher can
also be wrong on a training example, making unconditional imitation
undesirable. Finally, single-seed comparisons and unmatched schedules can
confound small reported improvements.

\textbf{The unanswered KD question is not only what to transfer, but where to
constrain and where to release.} J1 is the project name for a selective
hierarchical geometry-distillation objective. It combines a global
teacher-partition signal, an early relational anchor, balanced PC1--32 spatial
coordinates at selected transition and late states, and relational flow after
semantic maturity. It intentionally leaves the E1-to-M1 bridge without direct
teacher matching and turns off spatial losses during the final four epochs.
The design is related to feature, attention, relational, cross-layer, and
boundary-based KD, but its specific contribution is the selection and
calibration of complementary geometric constraints.

The choice of transition layers is empirical. Early, transition, and late
placement were tested during development. Early relational supervision was
useful, but dense coordinate or flow supervision too early constrained
immature features; using only the most compressed late state discarded
intermediate spatial organization; and applying constraints continuously
through the hierarchy performed worse than the selective configuration
\cite{chan2026j1}. The final taps therefore place coordinate supervision at
M1/M2, where spatial organization is becoming semantic, and use L1/L2 mainly
for mature geometry and affinity flow. This rationale is a result of the
development ablations, not a claim that every architecture has the same named
transition blocks.

J1 also addresses loss interaction. Its teacher-correct gate avoids imitating
unreliable teacher targets. PCA-band RMS balancing stops high-variance
coordinates from dominating lower-variance directions. Frozen random
hyperplanes transfer partitions rather than exact features. Gradient-ratio
calibration is related to work on adaptive multi-task weighting and gradient
conflict management \cite{chen2018gradnorm,kendall2018multitask,
sener2018multiobjective,yu2020pcgrad}; here it is used to freeze interpretable
training pressure before benchmark comparison. The current ImageNet-100 result
shows that the complete J1 package is a practical peer of ReviewKD, but matched
component, layer, cost, and generality experiments are still required.

\subsection{Vision transformers and future scope}

Vision Transformers (ViTs) replace convolutional feature hierarchies with
patch-token processing and global self-attention
\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit}. DeiT demonstrates data-efficient supervised
training and includes a distillation token, while Swin restores a hierarchical
multi-scale structure through shifted local windows
\cite{touvron2021deit,liu2021swin}. Comparative studies find different
representation and inductive-bias behavior between CNNs and ViTs
\cite{raghu2021vitcnn}, and self-supervised ViTs can develop salient
object-oriented attention structure without segmentation labels
\cite{caron2021dino}.

Attention weights are tempting explanations, but high attention is not in
general equivalent to feature importance or causal influence. Studies in
language models show that alternative attention distributions can preserve a
prediction and that removing high-attention items does not always produce the
largest change \cite{jain2019attention,serrano2019attention}; subsequent work
argues for more careful, claim-specific tests rather than a universal verdict
that attention is or is not explanation \cite{wiegreffe2019attention}.
Attention rollout and flow aggregate connectivity across layers
\cite{abnar2020attentionflow}. Transformer-specific explanation methods then
combine relevance propagation, causal interventions, token transformation, or
feature-formation gradients
\cite{chefer2021transformer,xie2023vitcx,wu2024tokentm,
mehri2025libragrad,bousselham2025legrad}.

The next phase will therefore begin with a focused literature review and a
small reproducible baseline set, not a fixed method claim. Candidate objects
include residual-stream states, patch tokens, value features, attention
outputs, and hierarchical stage features. \textbf{The transformer direction
will remain flexible until a measurement gate, semantic gate, and utility gate
identify a stable transition object.} A negative result for direct PCA-RGB or
K-DGRP transfer would still clarify which properties depend on convolutional
locality and which generalize across vision architectures.

\subsection{Synthesis of research gaps}

The literature leaves four gaps that define this PhD programme.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Formation gap.} Most post-hoc visual methods begin with a
    chosen output and layer; fewer studies characterize where object-preserving
    spatial features become class-organized across depth.
    \item \textbf{Explanation-object gap.} Object localization, class
    attribution, representation distinctiveness, and human usefulness are
    frequently compared as though they were interchangeable. They require
    explicit claim boundaries and multiple evaluation axes.
    \item \textbf{Selective-transfer gap.} KD provides many candidate
    knowledge objects, but layer placement, release of student-specific paths,
    and gradient-calibrated combination remain open design problems.
    \item \textbf{Architecture gap.} It is not yet established which
    representation-transition observations survive the change from CNN spatial
    grids to token-mixing architectures.
\end{enumerate}

The proposed flow follows these gaps: observe and quantify representation
transition; test its explanatory value through Adaptive K-DGRP; test its
training utility through J1; then examine its scope in transformer models. The
two immediate publication tasks are the K-DGRP and J1 papers, each with
placeholders in this report for the remaining confirmatory evidence.
