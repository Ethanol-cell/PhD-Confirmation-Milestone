\section{Progress II: Adaptive K-DGRP for Visual Explanation}
\label{sec:kdgrp}

\subsection{Research question and distinction from CAM}

Adaptive K Dataset-Global Robust Prototypes (K-DGRP) asks whether the
representation itself contains a class-agnostic geometric signal that identifies
object-relevant spatial locations \cite{chan2026kdgrp}. Unlike Grad-CAM, it does
not require a target class or a gradient. Unlike EigenCAM, it does not select a
dominant linear variance direction. Instead, K-DGRP estimates recurrent modes
of the dataset-wide spatial-feature distribution and measures how far each
location lies from those modes.

\textbf{K-DGRP and class activation mapping (CAM) answer different questions.}
K-DGRP measures prototype-relative representation distinctiveness and can be
evaluated for object-relevant localization. It does not, by itself, identify
which class caused a prediction. Class-conditioned CAMs are designed to
concentrate target-class evidence, whereas a class-agnostic map may cover a
broader part of the represented object. Localization and perturbation
faithfulness are therefore reported separately.

\subsection{Robust prototype geometry}

Let the selected semantic tap produce
\begin{equation}
    \bm F_\ell(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times H\times W},
\end{equation}
where $\bm f_p\in\mathbb{R}^D$ is the feature vector at spatial position $p$.
From label-free calibration images, collect the spatial-feature population
$\mathcal{F}=\{\bm f_n\}_{n=1}^N$. A single dataset-global robust prototype is
the geometric median
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \bm c^\star
    =\arg\min_{\bm c\in\mathbb{R}^D}
      \sum_{n=1}^N\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2.}
    \label{eq:dgrp-median}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Why use a geometric median?}

The arithmetic mean minimizes squared distance:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\bm f}
    =\arg\min_{\bm c}
      \sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Its gradient is
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_{\bm c}\sum_n\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2^2
    =2\sum_n(\bm c-\bm f_n).
\end{equation}
The contribution of one point therefore grows with
$\|\bm c-\bm f_n\|_2$. A very distant feature can pull the mean strongly.

For the unsquared objective
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(\bm c)=\sum_n\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2,
\end{equation}
the noncoincident contribution to the gradient is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\bm c-\bm f_n}{\|\bm c-\bm f_n\|_2},
\end{equation}
whose norm is one. A distant point can still affect the direction of the
solution, but it does not gain greater leverage merely because it is farther
away. \textbf{This matters because dataset-wide spatial features contain rare
parts, boundaries, atypical textures, and other extreme feature states.} The
reference mode should summarize recurrent feature structure without being
displaced strongly by a small number of extreme vectors.

\subsubsection{Implicit optimum and Weiszfeld iteration}

Away from a point of coincidence, the first-order condition is
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{n=1}^{N}
    \frac{\bm c^\star-\bm f_n}
         {\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2}=0.
    \label{eq:geomed-stationarity}
\end{equation}
Rearranging gives the fixed-point equation
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \bm c^\star=
    \frac{\displaystyle\sum_n
          \bm f_n/\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2}
         {\displaystyle\sum_n
          1/\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2}.}
    \label{eq:geomed-fixed-point}
\end{equation}
This expression characterizes the optimum but does not explicitly solve for it:
the unknown centre appears inside every inverse-distance weight. A generic
multidimensional geometric median therefore has no general
arithmetic-mean-like formula and is normally obtained iteratively; the
one-dimensional median and specially symmetric configurations are exceptions.

K-DGRP uses a stabilized Weiszfeld-type iteration
\cite{weiszfeld1937median}. In implementation-equivalent notation,
\begin{equation}
    w_n^{(t)}=
    \frac{1}{\max\!\left(\|\bm f_n-\bm c^{(t)}\|_2,\varepsilon\right)},
    \qquad
    \boxed{
    \bm c^{(t+1)}=
    \frac{\sum_n w_n^{(t)}\bm f_n}
         {\sum_n w_n^{(t)}}.}
    \label{eq:weiszfeld-update}
\end{equation}
Each iteration fixes the current inverse-distance weights and recomputes a
weighted centre. Recurrently populated regions exert strong aggregate influence
because many nearby vectors contribute in similar directions. An isolated
distant vector does not gain extra leverage merely from a large deviation.
Appendix~\ref{sec:kdgrp-weiszfeld-appendix} gives the fixed-point and
iteratively reweighted least-squares derivations.

The production fit starts from the arithmetic mean of all calibration spatial
vectors and uses $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$. A single-centre fit stops when
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\|\bm c^{(t+1)}-\bm c^{(t)}\|_2}
         {\max(1,\|\bm c^{(t)}\|_2)}<10^{-5},
    \label{eq:kdgrp-single-stop}
\end{equation}
or after 15 iterations. Distances, weights, and chunk reductions use FP32 on
the GPU. Cross-chunk accumulators use FP64 on the CPU. Coincident or
near-coincident vectors receive the capped weight $1/\varepsilon$; there is no
separate exact subgradient branch. For $K>1$, each assignment step is followed
by at most three centre updates. The procedure uses at most eight outer
iterations and an outer relative-shift threshold of $10^{-4}$. The resulting
finite-iteration centre banks are frozen; exact stationarity or a global
optimum is not asserted. Appendix~\ref{sec:kdgrp-weiszfeld-appendix} records
the complete initialization, reseeding, restart, and streaming procedure.

\subsubsection{Multiple robust modes and the explanation score}

For $K>1$, calibration vectors are assigned to their nearest centre,
\begin{equation}
    a_n=\arg\min_{k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}
        \|\bm f_n-\bm c_k\|_2,
\end{equation}
and each centre is updated as the geometric median of its assigned cluster,
\begin{equation}
    \bm c_k=\arg\min_{\bm c}
       \sum_{n:a_n=k}\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2.
\end{equation}
Assignment and robust-centre updates alternate until the frozen stopping rule
is reached. The joint multimode problem is not globally convex, so deterministic
initialization and calibration provenance are important for reproducibility.

Once the prototype bank is fitted, the raw explanation score is
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    R_K(p)=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}
        \|\bm f_p-\bm c_k\|_2.}
    \label{eq:kdgrp-score}
\end{equation}
\textbf{A high score means that the spatial representation is unusual relative
to every recurrent robust mode.} It is a measure of prototype-relative
representation distinctiveness, not a class probability, foreground
probability, anomaly probability, or distance to a class centroid.

A frozen histogram cumulative distribution function (CDF) maps the logarithm
of the nonnegative native-grid distances onto a common score scale. The
image-resolution map is
\begin{equation}
    \bm M=\mathcal U\!\left(F_{\mathrm{CDF}}
    (\bm R_{\mathrm{native}})\right),
    \label{eq:kdgrp-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
where CDF calibration is applied before bilinear upsampling $\mathcal U$ with
\texttt{align\_corners=False}. The nondecreasing CDF preserves weak ordering on
the native grid but may add ties. It does not guarantee the same
image-resolution top-area mask because calibration and interpolation do not
generally commute:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal U(F_{\mathrm{CDF}}(R))
    \neq F_{\mathrm{CDF}}(\mathcal U(R)).
\end{equation}
The CDF is therefore part of the frozen reconstruction pipeline rather than a
purely visual remapping. No final per-image minimum--maximum normalization is
applied. This clarification changes the description, not the evaluated method.

Controls that normalized by cluster radius, converted distance into a
cluster-conditional tail score, removed the prototype-relative reference, or
manually combined presumed foreground and background centres degraded
localization. \textbf{The canonical score therefore remains the raw Euclidean
distance in \cref{eq:kdgrp-score}.}

\subsection{Adaptive mode-count selection}

Late spatial-feature distributions are not equally multimodal across
architectures. A fixed $K$ that represents one backbone well can over-fragment
another. The final method considers $K\in\{1,2,4,8\}$. On a label-free held-out
calibration partition, cluster occupancy is
\begin{equation}
    \pi_k=\frac{N_k}{N},\qquad \sum_k\pi_k=1.
\end{equation}
Occupancy entropy, effective mode count, and utilization are
\begin{equation}
    H_K=-\sum_k\pi_k\log(\pi_k+\varepsilon),
    \qquad K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\exp(H_K),
    \qquad U_K=\frac{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}{K},
\end{equation}
using Shannon entropy to summarize occupancy balance
\cite{shannon1948entropy}. A larger candidate is admitted only when
\begin{equation}
    \min_k\pi_k\geq0.02,
    \qquad U_K\geq0.75.
    \label{eq:kselector}
\end{equation}
The search stops at the first failed candidate, after which the selected number
of centres is refitted on the full label-free calibration cohort. No object
mask or class label is used to choose $K$.

\textbf{Adaptive $K$ changes the number of reference modes without changing the
explanation principle.} The frozen registry selects $K=4$ for ResNet-50 and
$K=2$ for ConvNeXt-Base, VGG16-BN, RegNetY-8GF, MobileNetV3-Large, and
EfficientNetV2-S. These values must be read from the final manifest rather than
reselected from evaluation performance.

\subsection{Localization and prospective mechanism evidence}

On the original 600-image ResNet-50 cohort, K4-DGRP achieved intersection over
union at 20\% selected area (IoU@20) of $0.32946$ and area under the
precision--recall curve (AUPRC) of $0.69623$. Paired bootstrap differences in
IoU@20 (K-DGRP minus baseline) were $-0.002529$ against EigenCAM, 95\%
confidence interval (CI) $[-0.008263,0.003440]$, and $-0.001025$ against
Grad-CAM++, 95\% CI $[-0.006821,0.004865]$. The intervals crossed zero.
Grad-CAM and LayerCAM were higher by $0.009370$, 95\% CI
$[0.003211,0.015723]$, and $0.011319$, 95\% CI
$[0.005895,0.016888]$, respectively \cite{chan2026kdgrp}. These results are
treated as evidence of competitiveness rather than universal superiority.

\subsubsection{Prospective geometry-preservation control}

RANDOM64 is a controlled mechanism test, not a replacement explanation method.
Both features and frozen prototypes are embedded into the same fixed
64-dimensional random orthogonal subspace:
\begin{equation}
    \bm V\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times64},\qquad
    \bm V^\top\bm V=\bm I,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    R_{64}(p)=
    \min_k\|\bm V^\top(\bm f_p-\bm c_k)\|_2.
    \label{eq:kdgrp-random64}
\end{equation}

For this mechanism study, the faithfulness composite averages two benefits of
RANDOM64 relative to RAW: lower deletion AUC and higher insertion AUC. Each
image--method curve is normalized separately using its minimum and maximum
target logits, with the range floored at $10^{-12}$. Positive values favour
RANDOM64. The target is the clean predicted class and remains fixed across all
21 perturbation points. \textbf{This is a normalized-logit faithfulness
measure; it is not probability AUC for the target class.} Under this definition,
the composite difference is $-0.00093$ over 6,000 model--image observations.
Its 95\% CI is $[-0.00144,-0.00042]$, so it passes the frozen $-0.005$ margin.

Because the projection does not select a class direction, principal component,
or manually chosen semantic coordinate, the result shows that much of the
useful signal survives when prototype-relative Euclidean geometry is
approximately preserved.

\textbf{RANDOM64 remains slightly weaker than RAW and provides essentially no
end-to-end runtime improvement once projection cost is included.} It is retained
as mechanism evidence. The original full-dimensional RAW Adaptive K-DGRP
remains canonical.

\subsection{Mechanistic interpretation of K-DGRP}

\subsubsection{Decomposition of prototype-relative distance}

Let $\bm\mu$ be the frozen calibration feature mean, and define
\begin{equation}
    \bm h_p=\bm f_p-\bm\mu,
    \qquad
    \bm\delta_k=\bm c_k-\bm\mu.
\end{equation}
For the selected nearest robust mode
\begin{equation}
    k^\star(p)=\arg\min_k\|\bm f_p-\bm c_k\|_2,
\end{equation}
the squared score decomposes exactly as
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    R(p)^2=
    \underbrace{\|\bm h_p\|_2^2}_{\text{centred feature magnitude}}
    +\underbrace{\|\bm\delta_{k^\star}\|_2^2}_{\text{prototype location}}
    -\underbrace{2\bm h_p^\top\bm\delta_{k^\star}}_{
        \text{prototype-relative direction}}.}
    \label{eq:kdgrp-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The first term asks how far a feature lies from the global calibration centre.
The second depends on which recurrent mode is selected. The third measures the
directional alignment between the spatial feature and that mode.

Centred feature magnitude reproduces a substantial part of the RAW spatial
ordering, but it does not reproduce the complete K-DGRP result. Controls that
removed the prototype-relative reference degraded localization.
\textbf{Prototype location and prototype-relative direction therefore carry
information beyond global feature norm.}

\subsubsection{Why the method is robust: redundant Euclidean geometry}

A fixed random orthogonal projection does not deliberately preserve a class
direction, leading principal component, or manually selected coordinate. Each
retained coordinate mixes information from many original feature dimensions.
The prospective success of \cref{eq:kdgrp-random64} therefore indicates that
K-DGRP is not dependent on a small set of privileged axes.

\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \text{K-DGRP depends primarily on preserving
    prototype-relative Euclidean geometry.}}
\end{equation}
The useful geometry is high-dimensional, distributed, and redundant. Its exact
mode structure remains architecture dependent. \textbf{RANDOM64 demonstrates
redundancy of the geometry; it does not make random coordinates semantic.}

For a finite set of relevant feature--prototype pairs, random embedding theory
suggests that Euclidean distances can often be approximately preserved in fewer
dimensions \cite{johnson1984extensions}:
\begin{equation}
    (1-\eta)\|\bm f-\bm c_k\|_2^2
    \lesssim
    a\|\bm V^\top(\bm f-\bm c_k)\|_2^2
    \lesssim
    (1+\eta)\|\bm f-\bm c_k\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
This is an empirical connection to the principle behind
Johnson--Lindenstrauss embeddings, not a formal guarantee for the network
features in this study.

\subsubsection{Architecture differences and the neural-collapse boundary}

Architecture differences arise because late spatial-feature distributions
differ in multimodality and in how evenly useful distance information is spread
across feature dimensions. ResNet-50 uses four canonical robust modes in the
current registry and shows more projected prototype switching and lower
RAW-to-RANDOM64 map correlation than the $K=2$ architectures. This is an
observed association, not evidence that $K=4$ alone causes the behaviour.
All six architectures nevertheless remain within the prospective localization
non-inferiority margin. \textbf{The architecture-specific quantity is the exact
geometry of the recurrent modes; the architecture-general principle is distance
from those modes.}

This robust-mode geometry is distinct from neural collapse. Neural collapse
concerns pooled class means and classifier geometry, whereas Adaptive K-DGRP
models recurrent modes among unpooled spatial features
\cite{papyan2020neuralcollapse,zhu2021neuralcollapse,
han2022neuralcollapse,fang2021layerpeeled,kothapalli2023neuralcollapse}.
K-DGRP centres have low class purity and are far fewer than the number of
classes, so they should not be interpreted as class-simplex vertices. Neural
collapse remains useful for describing the maturation of late class geometry,
but it is not the mechanism that defines the K-DGRP centres. The supported
interpretation is narrower: object-relevant positions tend to exhibit greater
prototype-relative feature distinctiveness.

\subsection{Remaining evidence and publication scope}

\textbf{The mechanistic search over alternative K-DGRP scoring formulas is
closed for the current CNN method.} The canonical explanation remains raw
Euclidean distance to the nearest adaptive robust mode. The completed
prospective control supplies perturbation-faithfulness evidence for the
mechanism; continued PCA, radius-normalized, common-gain, or residual-score
search is not required for the milestone claim.

Remaining work should focus on publication packaging, external cross-dataset
generalization, unfinished parameter/label-randomization or class-sensitivity
controls, and a fixed qualitative semantic atlas that includes both successes
and failures. These studies define the boundary between class-agnostic
object-relevant localization and target-class attribution. They do not reopen
the canonical score.

The v6 audit verifies the primary 580-image localization results and saved
paired-statistics artifacts against their recorded pre-prospective hashes. The
secondary prospective cohort contains 890 images. Localization uses 888 valid
paired observations for ConvNeXt-Base, VGG16-BN, and MobileNetV3-Large and 890
for the other architectures; all 890 remain eligible for mask-free
faithfulness and RemOve And Debias (ROAD) evaluation. Six prospective
full-curve model outputs and four of six ImageNet-100 mask-free outputs are
present. However, their completion markers do not contain historical content
hashes, and the saved AUC summaries integrate logits rather than target-class
probabilities.

\textbf{Progress II is therefore not reported as final.} Probability-AUC
repair, ROAD, parameter and label randomization, final prospective statistics,
and the semantic/error atlas remain open. The separate RANDOM64 study supports
pooled localization non-inferiority and its explicitly defined normalized-logit
faithfulness criterion, but provides no meaningful end-to-end speed benefit.
RAW full-dimensional Adaptive K-DGRP remains canonical.

The intended publication claim is deliberately precise:
\textbf{Adaptive K-DGRP is a class-agnostic, gradient-free representation
explanation that localizes object-relevant spatial structure through Euclidean
distinctiveness from adaptive recurrent robust feature modes.} It should not be
summarized as universally outperforming CAMs or as identifying the class-causal
pixels for a prediction.
