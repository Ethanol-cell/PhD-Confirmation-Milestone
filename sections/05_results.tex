\section{Progress II: Adaptive K-DGRP for Visual Explanation}
\label{sec:kdgrp}

\subsection{Research question and distinction from CAM}

\methodname{} asks whether the representation itself contains a
class-agnostic geometric signal that identifies object-relevant spatial
locations \cite{chan2026kdgrp}. Unlike Grad-CAM, it does not require a target
class or a gradient. Unlike EigenCAM, it does not select the dominant linear
variance direction. It estimates recurrent modes of the dataset-wide spatial
feature distribution and measures how far each image location lies from those
modes.

This distinction controls the claims. \methodname{} is naturally interpreted
as representation distinctiveness or object-relevant localization. It cannot by
itself answer which class caused a prediction. Its localization results can be
compared with CAMs, but a smaller deletion effect is not necessarily a failure:
class-conditioned CAMs are designed to concentrate causal class evidence,
whereas a class-agnostic map may cover more of the object.

\subsection{Robust prototype geometry}

Let the selected semantic tap produce
\begin{equation}
    \bm F_\ell(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times H\times W},
\end{equation}
with spatial vector $\bm f_p\in\mathbb{R}^D$. Across label-free calibration
images, collect all spatial vectors
$\mathcal{F}=\{\bm f_n\}_{n=1}^N$. A single dataset-global robust prototype
is the geometric median
\begin{equation}
    \bm c^\star
    =\arg\min_{\bm c\in\mathbb{R}^D}
      \sum_{n=1}^N\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2.
    \label{eq:dgrp-median}
\end{equation}
which can be computed by Weiszfeld-type iterations \cite{weiszfeld1937median}.
Compared with an arithmetic mean, the unsquared objective limits the influence
of rare, distant spatial vectors.

The multimodal extension borrows the alternating assignment/update structure of
prototype clustering \cite{macqueen1967kmeans}: it alternates nearest-centre assignment
\begin{equation}
    a_n=\arg\min_{k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}
        \|\bm f_n-\bm c_k\|_2
\end{equation}
with geometric-median updates inside each assigned cluster. The raw explanation
score is
\begin{equation}
    R_K(p)=\min_{1\leq k\leq K}
        \|\bm f_p-\bm c_k\|_2.
    \label{eq:kdgrp-score}
\end{equation}
A high value means that the spatial feature is far from every recurrent robust
mode. An empirical calibration CDF can map this score to $[0,1]$ for display,
but its monotonicity preserves exact top-area masks and rank metrics.

The experiments found that normalizing by cluster radius, converting distance
to a cluster-conditional tail score, or manually combining presumed
foreground/background centres reduced localization. Mode scale was itself
informative: broad modes were more foreground-associated. Raw distance in
\cref{eq:kdgrp-score} therefore survived the ablation sequence.

\subsection{Adaptive mode-count selection}

Fixed $K=4$ worked well for ResNet-50 but over-fragmented ConvNeXt features.
The final method considers $K\in\{1,2,4,8\}$. On a held-out calibration
partition, cluster occupancy is
\begin{equation}
    \pi_k=\frac{N_k}{N},\qquad \sum_k\pi_k=1.
\end{equation}
Occupancy entropy and effective mode count are
\begin{equation}
    H_K=-\sum_k\pi_k\log(\pi_k+\varepsilon),
    \qquad K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\exp(H_K),
\end{equation}
using Shannon entropy to summarize occupancy balance
\cite{shannon1948entropy}, with utilization $U_K=K_{\mathrm{eff}}/K$. Starting at $K=1$, a larger
candidate is admitted only when
\begin{equation}
    \min_k\pi_k\geq0.02,
    \qquad U_K\geq0.75.
    \label{eq:kselector}
\end{equation}
The search stops at the first failure, then the selected number of centres is
refitted on the full calibration cohort. The selector controls complexity
without segmentation masks or class labels.

\subsection{Completed internal evidence}

On a 600-image ResNet-50 cohort, K4-DGRP achieved IoU@20 of $0.32946$ and
AUPRC of $0.69623$. Paired bootstrap differences in IoU@20 (K-DGRP minus
baseline) were $-0.002529$ against EigenCAM, 95\% CI
$[-0.008263,0.003440]$, and $-0.001025$ against Grad-CAM++, 95\% CI
$[-0.006821,0.004865]$. The intervals crossed zero. Grad-CAM and LayerCAM were
higher by $0.009370$, 95\% CI $[0.003211,0.015723]$, and $0.011319$, 95\% CI
$[0.005895,0.016888]$, respectively \cite{chan2026kdgrp}. The supplied internal
report does not document multiplicity adjustment, so these intervals are
treated as descriptive evidence of competitiveness rather than a complete
confirmatory test.

The selector chose $K=2$ for all four previously unseen models. Its mean
post-hoc oracle regret was $0.000147$ IoU and maximum regret was $0.000350$.
Its point estimate exceeded EigenCAM on all four models. It was close to the
gradient CAMs on VGG, exceeded Grad-CAM on MobileNet, and trailed the strongest
gradient CAMs on RegNet, MobileNet, and EfficientNetV2. These are point-estimate
comparisons; the corresponding per-model paired intervals are not reproduced
here. Robust-distance maps from independently trained architectures also
showed same-image spatial Spearman correlations ranging from approximately
$0.55$ to $0.79$ for the reported pairs.

These results indicate that robust distance is more architecture-general than a
fixed mode count. They do not yet establish cross-dataset generalization,
class-specific attribution, or complete causal faithfulness.

\subsection{Neural collapse and interpretation}

Late ResNet features became increasingly class-organized: within-class to
between-class scatter decreased, simplex error decreased, and nearest-class
centre accuracy increased toward the final stage. Nevertheless, K-DGRP centres
were not class-simplex vertices. For 100 classes the centred class means span up
to 99 dimensions, while four robust centres describe a much lower-cardinality
spatial/common-mode geometry. Centre class purity was low at useful $K$, and
class-only or residual-only alternatives underperformed raw distance.

\textbf{This is related to, but not an instance of, neural collapse.} The
terminal class-centre geometry studied in neural-collapse work concerns pooled
or sample-level class representations, whereas K-DGRP models recurrent modes
among unpooled spatial vectors. The distinction explains why a small $K$ can be
useful even when the classifier has 100 classes
\cite{papyan2020neuralcollapse,zhu2021neuralcollapse,
han2022neuralcollapse,fang2021layerpeeled,kothapalli2023neuralcollapse}.

The current interpretation is therefore that discriminative training creates a
late feature space containing both class-semantic organization and broad
recurrent spatial modes. Object interiors, parts, and boundaries are more
variable than common background or context and often lie farther from the
nearest robust mode. The explanation score captures this structured novelty
rather than distance from a class centroid.
