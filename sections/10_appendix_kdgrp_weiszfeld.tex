\section{Geometric-Median Prototypes and the Weiszfeld Solver}
\label{sec:kdgrp-weiszfeld-appendix}

This appendix makes the robust-prototype calculation independently
understandable. Mathematical identities are separated from implementation
choices. The implementation details below are taken from the frozen v6
production audit. Where historical execution metadata are incomplete, that
limitation is stated explicitly.

\subsection{Arithmetic mean versus geometric median}

For calibration features $\{\bm f_n\}_{n=1}^{N}$, the arithmetic mean minimizes
the squared-distance objective
\begin{equation}
    J_2(\bm c)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Differentiating gives
\begin{equation}
    \nabla J_2(\bm c)
    =2N\bm c-2\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bm f_n.
\end{equation}
Setting the gradient to zero yields the explicit solution
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\bm f}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bm f_n.
\end{equation}
Because the objective squares each residual, the magnitude of one point's
gradient contribution grows linearly with its distance from the current centre.

The geometric median instead minimizes
\begin{equation}
    J_1(\bm c)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2.
\end{equation}
For $\bm c\neq\bm f_n$, each contribution satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \left\|
    \frac{\bm c-\bm f_n}{\|\bm c-\bm f_n\|_2}
    \right\|_2=1.
\end{equation}
Thus, an extreme point is not ignored, but its influence does not grow merely
because it is farther from the centre. This property motivates its use as a
robust reference for a calibration population containing rare spatial-feature
states.

\subsection{First-order condition and the implicit solution}

When the candidate centre does not coincide with an observation,
\begin{equation}
    \nabla J_1(\bm c)
    =\sum_{n=1}^{N}
      \frac{\bm c-\bm f_n}{\|\bm c-\bm f_n\|_2}.
\end{equation}
At a differentiable optimum $\bm c^\star$,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_n
    \frac{\bm c^\star-\bm f_n}
         {\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2}=0.
\end{equation}
Moving the feature terms to the right and collecting $\bm c^\star$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \bm c^\star
    \sum_n\frac{1}{\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2}
    =
    \sum_n\frac{\bm f_n}{\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2},
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \bm c^\star=
    \frac{\displaystyle\sum_n
          \bm f_n/\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2}
         {\displaystyle\sum_n
          1/\|\bm c^\star-\bm f_n\|_2}.}
\end{equation}
The unknown centre occurs inside every denominator, so this is a nonlinear
fixed-point equation rather than an explicit solution. A generic
multidimensional geometric median has no general arithmetic-mean-like formula
and is normally solved iteratively. This statement allows the familiar
one-dimensional median and specially symmetric configurations as exceptions.

\subsection{Weiszfeld fixed-point and IRLS derivation}

Weiszfeld iteration replaces the unknown weights with weights computed from the
current iterate:
\begin{equation}
    w_n^{(t)}=
    \frac{1}{\max\!\left(\|\bm f_n-\bm c^{(t)}\|_2,\varepsilon\right)},
    \qquad
    \bm c^{(t+1)}=
    \frac{\sum_nw_n^{(t)}\bm f_n}{\sum_nw_n^{(t)}}.
\end{equation}
The production implementation uses the capped inverse distance above with
$\varepsilon=10^{-6}$. It therefore agrees with the ordinary Weiszfeld weight
when the distance is at least $\varepsilon$ and remains finite near a
coincident point.

The same update can be understood as iteratively reweighted least squares
(IRLS). Let
\begin{equation}
    r_n^{(t)}=\|\bm f_n-\bm c^{(t)}\|_2.
\end{equation}
For $r_n^{(t)}>0$, the tangent upper bound for the square root gives
\begin{equation}
    \|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2
    \leq
    \frac{\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2^2}{2r_n^{(t)}}
    +\frac{r_n^{(t)}}{2}.
\end{equation}
Summing these bounds produces the quadratic surrogate
\begin{equation}
    Q(\bm c\mid\bm c^{(t)})
    =\sum_n
      \frac{\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2^2}{2r_n^{(t)}}
      +\text{constant}.
\end{equation}
Minimizing this weighted least-squares problem yields
\begin{equation}
    \bm c^{(t+1)}=
    \frac{\sum_n\bm f_n/r_n^{(t)}}
         {\sum_n1/r_n^{(t)}}.
\end{equation}
The stabilized implementation prevents a singular inverse distance when an
iterate approaches an observation.

\subsection{Production initialization}

For $N$ calibration spatial vectors, the single-centre initialization is
\begin{equation}
    \bm c^{(0)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bm f_n.
    \label{eq:kdgrp-production-init}
\end{equation}
All calibration vectors contribute. The implementation streams the frozen
feature cache in image order, promotes FP16 cache values to FP32, sums each
chunk in FP32, and accumulates chunk totals and the vector count on the CPU in
FP64. The selected taps use fixed 64-image chunks. The ResNet-50 initializer is
the archived Campaign-71 centre reused by Campaign 72 and then byte-identically
by Campaign 75. ConvNeXt-Base and the four Campaign-80 architectures use their
respective calibration fits. This arithmetic mean initializes the iterative
solver; it does not replace the geometric-median objective.

\subsection{Numerical stabilization and stopping}

The implemented update is
\begin{equation}
    r_n^{(t)}=\|\bm f_n-\bm c^{(t)}\|_2,
    \qquad
    w_n^{(t)}=\frac{1}{\max(r_n^{(t)},10^{-6})},
\end{equation}
followed by the weighted mean in the previous subsection. The single-centre
stopping test is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\|\bm c^{(t+1)}-\bm c^{(t)}\|_2}
         {\max(1,\|\bm c^{(t)}\|_2)}<10^{-5},
\end{equation}
with a maximum of 15 iterations and a strict comparison. Distances, weights,
and chunk reductions use FP32 on the GPU; cross-chunk accumulators use FP64 on
the CPU. For a cluster update, the denominator of the accumulated weighted
mean is additionally clamped below by $10^{-6}$. Multimode centres are cast
back to FP32 after each update. Coincident and near-coincident vectors receive
the capped weight $10^6$; no separate exact subgradient branch or early return
is implemented.

The unstabilized fixed-point equation is exact only for noncoincident vectors.
Where the cap is active, the production solver instead follows a capped-weight
approximation. Its finite iteration limit also means that the saved centre is a
reproducible numerical estimate, not a claim of exact stationarity.

\subsection{Extension to multiple modes}

For $K>1$, the frozen method alternates between nearest-centre assignment,
\begin{equation}
    a_n=\arg\min_k\|\bm f_n-\bm c_k\|_2,
\end{equation}
and a geometric-median update within each assigned cluster,
\begin{equation}
    \bm c_k=\arg\min_{\bm c}
      \sum_{n:a_n=k}\|\bm f_n-\bm c\|_2.
\end{equation}
The joint assignment-and-centre objective is not globally convex. The
production initialization, tie handling, empty-cluster policy, and stopping
rule therefore matter for reproducibility.

For each $K\in\{2,4,8\}$, the first centre is the frozen single-centre estimate.
Additional centres are drawn with probability proportional to squared distance
from the nearest existing centre on a seed pool of at most 65,536 calibration
vectors. The predetermined seeds are 20260818, 20260819, and 20260820. NumPy's
default PCG64 generator samples pool indices without replacement, after which
the indices are sorted. Each value of $K$ is initialized independently from the
same single centre; a lower-$K$ solution does not warm-start a higher-$K$ fit.

Assignments minimize FP32 squared Euclidean distance. An exact computed tie is
resolved in favour of the lowest centre index. Each assigned cluster begins its
inner solve from its current centre. At most three inner Weiszfeld updates and
eight outer assignment--update iterations are used. The maximum relative
centre shift must be strictly below $10^{-5}$ for the inner solve and
$10^{-4}$ for the outer solve, with each denominator bounded below by one.

A cluster whose occupancy is below $0.0025$ is reseeded deterministically from
high-error seed-pool candidates after a provisional support check. Each
seed--$K$ fit permits at most two reseeding rounds; a further failure marks the
fit as collapsed. Among surviving fits, the smallest full-calibration sum of
Euclidean distances is retained, with the numerical seed breaking an objective
tie. ResNet-50 retains seed 20260818 and ConvNeXt-Base retains seed 20260819.
The four Campaign-80 full-refit banks do not preserve the winning-seed metadata,
so their individual winning seeds remain unverified even though the three-seed
policy is traceable.

Fixed vector order, row-major spatial flattening, and chunk size are part of the
reproducibility conditions. The inspected Campaign-72 multimode stream uses
256-image chunks, whereas the Campaign-71 single-centre stream and Campaign
79/80 streams use 64-image chunks. Binding the currently inspected source
version to every historical execution remains an audit limitation. GPU FP32
indexed accumulation also does not establish cross-hardware bitwise
determinism. This alternating procedure has no global-optimum guarantee, and
its finite iteration limits do not certify an exact stationary configuration.

\subsection{Connection to the explanation score}

The solver is used only during label-free calibration to estimate recurrent
reference modes. Once these modes are frozen, explanation inference uses
\begin{equation}
    R_K(p)=\min_k\|\bm f_p-\bm c_k\|_2.
\end{equation}
No iterative optimization is required for an individual image. Inference needs
only feature extraction, distances to the frozen centres, nearest-mode
selection, calibration through the frozen CDF, and spatial reconstruction.
